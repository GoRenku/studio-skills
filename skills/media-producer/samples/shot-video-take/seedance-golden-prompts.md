# Seedance Golden Prompt Samples

Use these as shape examples for final `shot.video-take` prompts. Adapt to the
actual take context, provider preview, route, duration, and user direction. Do
not copy names, places, actions, or reference tokens blindly.

Only name tokens that the final provider preview actually supplies. For a
realistic storyboard that already absorbed its upstream location/look/character
references, do not add `@Image2` or `@Image3` just because those images were
used during storyboard generation. For a hand-drawn or abstract storyboard,
extra image references can be appropriate when they provide final appearance.

## Text-Only Establishing Shot

```text
One continuous eight-second establishing shot of a rain-dark market arcade at
night after curfew. The street is empty except for wind pushing wet hanging
cloth signs and puddles trembling under runoff from the roof tiles.

Camera and motion: begin at knee height outside the arcade, then make a slow
dolly forward under three stone arches. Keep the movement steady and cautious,
with slight handheld weight. Let the foreground arch pass overhead, then drift
past a row of shuttered stalls, ending on a closed wooden door with warm light
leaking beneath it.

Rhythm: quiet, patient, and tense. The shot should feel like the camera is
listening for danger, not rushing toward an action beat.

Sound: soft rain, distant crowd murmur far beyond the arcade, water dripping
from cloth awnings, one cloth sign snapping once in the wind near the midpoint.

Look and continuity: muted green and amber palette, wet stone, practical
period lantern glow, natural handheld realism, no modern signage or electric
lights.

Do not include subtitles, logos, vehicles, modern wires, neon, extra
characters, text overlays, or a dramatic musical score.
```

## Image-To-Video / First Frame

```text
@Image1 is the opening frame. Begin from its exact composition, character
identity, wardrobe, prop shape, doorway geometry, crowd placement, and light
direction.

Animate one restrained motion arc: Elise raises the torn petition from chest
height into the doorway light while her shoulders tighten and her breath
catches. The crowd behind her shifts slightly, but nobody steps forward or
changes position enough to steal focus.

Camera and motion: a subtle handheld push-in that keeps shallow focus on her
eyes and the paper edge. The petition should move through real hand, wrist, and
elbow motion, not by morphing or sliding as a flat image.

Rhythm: quiet pressure building in a single breath. Start nearly still, let the
paper rise slowly, and hold the final raised position for the last beat.

Sound: crowd chant softens under her breath; paper fibers rustle as the petition
enters the light.

Keep the market doorway layout, petition design, costume, period details, and
background geography stable. Do not add text overlays, new characters, new
props, changed wardrobe, or a different background.
```

## First-And-Last Frame

```text
@Image1 is the first frame. Start from its exact subject identity, composition,
market doorway layout, light direction, torn petition, crowd placement, and
wardrobe.

@Image2 is the required final frame. End at its closer composition with the
petition raised into the doorway light.

Transition: Elise steps half a pace forward and raises the petition through
real body and arm movement. Keep identity, prop design, crowd placement, and
doorway geography continuous. The change should happen through one restrained
handheld push-in, not by morphing the architecture or sliding the first frame
into the second.

Rhythm: start still, move on her inhale, and settle into the final pose with a
short held silence.

Sound: crowd ambience drops as the paper catches light; a soft cloth rustle
from her sleeve; no music swell.

Do not include subtitles, new props, extra characters, changed wardrobe,
architecture morphing, or a different time of day.
```

## Storyboard Reference, Realistic Continuous Establishing Shot With Narration

```text
REFERENCES
@Image1 is the finished cinematic storyboard blueprint for this 15-second
video. Read it as six ordered waypoints in one uninterrupted drone movement,
not as a first frame, not as a collage, and not as a page layout to copy. The
storyboard is realistic/final-style and already contains the location,
geography, period look, palette, and lighting needed for this final video. Do
not add or request extra image references for location or look.

@Audio1 is the narrator voice/style reference. Use its voice character for the
spoken narration below.

CRITICAL STORYBOARD RULE
Each waypoint controls camera angle, shot scale, framing, geography, screen
direction, motion direction, movement pace, rhythm, and narration timing. Turn
the panels into continuous high-quality cinematic footage in order. Do not show
storyboard borders, arrows, labels, captions, timing rows, metadata, UI, panel
grid, text boxes, or page layout.

OVERALL CAMERA AND RHYTHM
One continuous aerial drone shot at grim dawn, period-realistic 1453 siege.
The rhythm starts suspended and watchful over the city, gathers forward pull
toward the wall, opens into a brief empty breath over the killing ground, then
descends with increasing weight toward the cannon before a final quick whip/drop
into the cannon mouth. No hard cuts. No split screen. No collage. No panorama of
panels. Render final cinematic realism, not storyboard art.

WAYPOINTS AS MOTION
Waypoint 1, seconds 0-2: high overhead inside Christian Byzantine
Constantinople. Hagia Sophia appears as a Byzantine church form among period
rooftops and narrow streets. Smoke rises slowly from damaged quarters while
tiny distressed people move below in streets and courtyards. The drone begins a
smooth outward glide from the city interior, almost floating, with the city
opening beneath the camera. Wordless opening. No mosques, no minarets, no later
Ottoman skyline, no Ottoman architecture inside the city, no cannon yet.

Waypoint 2, seconds 2-4: still high aerial, continuing the outward glide from
the city toward the Theodosian Walls. The city remains behind and below the
camera path as the walls grow larger ahead. Defenders become visible as tiny
moving figures along parapets and towers. The drone movement becomes more
purposeful but remains smooth. The narrator begins here.

Waypoint 3, seconds 4-7: the drone crosses over the wall from the city side to
the battlefield side in one coherent forward movement. The wall must clearly
separate Christian Constantinople behind from empty ground ahead. Keep the
geography continuous from Waypoint 2 into Waypoint 3: no side approach, no
flipped direction, no swapped city and battlefield. Tiny defenders remain on
the battlements as the camera passes over them. Smoke and dawn haze slide
backward beneath the drone. The narration phrase "These walls had taught
armies to turn back" spans this crossing and continues into Waypoint 4.

Waypoint 4, seconds 7-9: the camera is now outside the walls, over the empty
churned killing ground. The walls are no longer visible because the drone has
already crossed them. The field immediately below is mostly empty: scarred
earth, tracks, dust, and smoke drifting low. The Ottoman encampment sits far in
the distance ahead. Continue forward toward the distant camp, with a brief
rhythmic hush after the wall crossing. The narrator completes: "These walls had
taught armies to turn back."

Waypoint 5, seconds 9-12: descend toward a single huge bronze Ottoman bombard
in the siege camp. The camera lowers and accelerates slightly, making the cannon
become the dominant object ahead. Show heavy bronze, ropes, carts, crews, tents,
soldiers, and camp labor behind and around it. Dust rises from feet and wheels;
ropes creak under strain. The narrator begins the second line here: "A new
lesson arrived..."

Waypoint 6, seconds 12-15: make a quick whip/drop move from top-down aerial
alignment into a low frontal alignment with the bronze cannon mouth. The dark
bore fills the foreground at the end, imposing and close. Ottoman camp,
soldiers, ropes, carts, and tents sit behind and around the cannon. Finish the
narration here: "...on wheels." No city or walls behind the cannon, no second
cannon, no firing, no explosion, no muzzle flash.

NARRATION AND AUDIO
The narrator says exactly: "These walls had taught armies to turn back. A new
lesson arrived on wheels."

Audio timing target: narration starts during Waypoint 2; "These walls had
taught armies to turn back" spans Waypoints 3 and 4; "A new lesson arrived on
wheels" spans Waypoints 5 and 6.

Under the voice: restrained dawn wind, distant bells and city murmur early,
then the sound field transitions to empty ground wind, camp labor, rope creaks,
muffled Ottoman orders, animals, and faint bronze/metal settling near the
cannon.

CRITICAL EXCLUSIONS
No modern objects, no graphic overlays, no written text, no visible storyboard
marks, no page artifacts, no hard cuts, no split screen, no collage, no
panorama of panels, no morphing geography, no minarets or mosques in
Constantinople, no city or walls behind the final cannon mouth.
```

## Storyboard Reference, Edited Hand-Drawn Action Scene

```text
REFERENCES
@Image1 is the hand-drawn action storyboard. Use it for shot order, staging,
camera angle, shot scale, body mechanics, screen direction, action rhythm, and
motion cues only. Do not render its sketch texture, blank mannequin qualities,
panel borders, arrows, labels, captions, timing rows, or page layout.

@Image2 is the final appearance reference for C1: the slim long-haired robed
hero. Use it for face, hair, body type, wardrobe, cloth weight, and color.

@Image3 is the final appearance reference for C2: the broad monk opponent. Use
it for face, build, robe construction, skin, posture, and wardrobe.

CRITICAL STORYBOARD RULE
Turn @Image1 into an edited cinematic martial-arts sequence in the exact panel
order. The panels are separate shots with fast cuts, not one continuous camera
path and not a collage. All arrows and energy marks describe movement only;
none should appear as storyboard graphics.

VISUAL MEDIUM
Cinematic 3D realism with stylized martial-arts energy, detailed skin, cloth
weight, grounded body contact, and feature-film finish.

MOTION LANGUAGE
Explosive close-quarters kung fu: Wing Chun centerline pressure, trapping
hands, circular redirects, real body weight, cloth lag, and one brief impact
speed ramp at Shot 7. Every strike must read as body mechanics first, aura
second.

RHYTHM AND ESCALATION
Shots 1-2 advance sharply, Shots 3-6 accelerate through counters and traps,
Shot 7 slows for one brutal chest contact, and Shot 8 holds the empty release.
The edit should feel percussive: fast contact, shorter breaths between beats,
then one suspended impact moment and hard quiet.

Shot 1 / Panel 1: low floor-level wide from the center lane, floor filling the
lower frame as C1 drives screen left to right into C2. C2 braces from screen
right. Camera tracks backward just ahead of C1's advance. Forearm bands are
fast aura trails attached to the limbs, not physical objects.

Shot 2 / Panel 2: tight over-C2-shoulder medium close. C1 crowds the lens, pins
C2's wrist across the centerline, and snaps her other fist toward his chest.
The camera pushes in a few inches with the punch. Violet and gold energy wraps
the trapped wrist and punch path from skin contact.

Shot 3 / Panel 3: canted side medium. C2 sweeps a wide circular deflection from
screen right, turning C1's straight punch outward while stepping around her
lead foot. Camera whips laterally with the redirect. Teal and crimson arcs tear
from forearm and heel pivot as shockwave dust skims the floor.

Shot 4 / Panel 4: extreme close insert on hands and sternum line. C1 slaps
C2's guard down and threads two chain punches through the gap. Make the hands
precise and readable, with small aura bursts popping from knuckles and
forearms. Cloth fibers and dust kick loose on each contact.

Shot 5 / Panel 5: bird's-eye wide. C1 steps inside the circle in a tight
triangular stance near center left while C2 swings a heavy arm outside at
center right. The circular floor streak is displaced dust and aura drag from
footwork. Camera rotates a few degrees with the footwork to show the trap.

Shot 6 / Panel 6: compressed profile medium. C1 is inside C2's guard at screen
left, one checking hand pinning the outer arm while her striking palm chambers
near C2's chest. C2's late counter hangs behind his shoulder. Small teal and
gold currents snap between wrists. Hold half a beat before the strike.

Shot 7 / Panel 7: low chest-impact close. C1 plants the decisive palm strike
into C2's chest, bodies locked in silhouette. Use one brief speed ramp at the
instant of contact, then nearly freeze the bodies as a luminous spirit
silhouette erupts from C2's back as an impact-attached energy event.
Multicolor shockwaves fold outward from the contact point.

Shot 8 / Panel 8: steady rear-wide release from behind C2's collapsing body.
C1 stands screen left with palm lowered as C2 folds toward the stone floor at
screen right. The spirit shape is gone. Let fading particles, dust rings,
settling cloth, and hard silence carry the ending.

AUDIO
No background music. Breath, foot slides, cloth snaps, palm cracks, aura
crackle, floor impacts, debris ticks, one chest-hit thunderclap at Shot 7, then
hard silence for Shot 8.

ENVIRONMENT
Worn night training hall: stone floor, two pillar rows, hanging cloth strips,
open center lane, suspended dust, practical low-key light.

CRITICAL EXCLUSIONS
No storyboard borders, labels, arrows, captions, panel grid, UI, text overlays,
floating annotation marks, extra fighters, weapons, comedic physics, or
rubbery morphing limbs.
```

## Storyboard Reference, Dialogue Scene With Audio References

```text
REFERENCES
@Image1 is the realistic four-panel storyboard for this 12-second dialogue
take. It controls shot order, framing, eyelines, blocking, hand movement,
camera pace, and the emotional rhythm. Do not treat it as a first frame and do
not copy the page layout.

@Audio1 is Mara's speaker voice reference: low, controlled, close-mic, carrying
fatigue under restraint.

@Audio2 is Elias's speaker voice reference: quieter, older, rougher, with a
small pause before answering.

CRITICAL STORYBOARD RULE
Turn the four panels into an edited cinematic dialogue sequence. Preserve
eyelines, table geography, candle position, and screen direction across cuts.
Do not show panel borders, arrows, labels, captions, subtitles, metadata rows,
UI, or any written text from the storyboard.

SCENE AND LOOK
A candlelit monastery map room before dawn. Two characters sit across a rough
wood table covered with a folded campaign map, wax drips, and a single iron
key. The room is quiet, stone-walled, and cold. The camera language is
restrained and intimate: small moves, shallow focus, candle flicker, controlled
breathing, no melodrama.

Shot 1 / Panel 1, seconds 0-3: quiet two-shot from the end of the table. Mara
sits screen left in three-quarter profile, Elias screen right, the candle
between them. The camera makes a very slow push toward the table. Mara's hand
hovers near the iron key but does not touch it. No dialogue yet. Sound is room
tone, candle tick, distant bell fading outside.

Shot 2 / Panel 2, seconds 3-6: medium close on Mara over Elias's shoulder.
Mara finally touches the key with two fingers but does not pick it up. Her eyes
stay on Elias, not the prop. Camera holds steady with tiny handheld breath.
Mara says exactly: "You heard the bells before sunrise."

Shot 3 / Panel 3, seconds 6-9: reverse medium close on Elias over Mara's
shoulder. Elias looks down to the map, then back to Mara. His answer starts
after a short pause. Candlelight trembles across his face; his hand folds the
map edge inward by one inch. Elias says exactly: "No. I heard the silence after
them."

Shot 4 / Panel 4, seconds 9-12: return to the two-shot, slightly closer than
Shot 1. Mara's hand closes around the key but stays on the table. Elias leans
back a fraction, not away from her, but away from the decision. Let the final
two seconds hold in silence with candle flicker and distant dawn wind.

DIALOGUE TIMING TARGET
Mara's line belongs entirely to Shot 2. Elias's line begins after a small pause
early in Shot 3 and completes before Shot 4. Shot 4 is silent reaction and
decision. Mouth movement should feel natural to the spoken lines, but do not
add subtitles or on-screen dialogue text.

AUDIO BED
Under the voices: candle tick, low room tone, faint wind outside stone, one
distant bell decay before the first line. No music. No crowd. No exaggerated
reverb.

CRITICAL EXCLUSIONS
No subtitles, no visible script text, no extra characters, no modern lamps, no
changed table layout, no swapped eyelines, no page layout, no storyboard
artifacts, no sudden camera whip, no action beat beyond the key and map
gestures.
```
